Privacy policy

What data we keep, why, for how long, who sees it and what rights you have.

This is an English translation provided for convenience. If it differs from the Greek text, the Greek text applies.

Last updated: 5 October 2026

This policy explains how the Association processes personal data through the website semfealumni.gr, in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (Regulation (EU) 2016/679, “GDPR”), Greek Law 4624/2019 and Greek Law 3471/2006 on the protection of privacy in electronic communications.

In short

  • You can read the whole website without an account. We use no cookies and no advertising, and we do not build profiles of visitors.
  • We count visits only as totals, without keeping your IP address.
  • If you create an account or apply for membership, we keep what is needed for the register of members and what you choose to give us.
  • We do not sell your details and we do not give them to anyone for advertising.
  • You can see, correct and delete your details yourself, from the My account page.

Who is responsible

The data controller is the SEMFE Alumni Association (“the Association”), the non-profit association of the graduates of the School of Applied Mathematical and Physical Sciences of the National Technical University of Athens, registered in Greece as «Σύλλογος Διπλωματούχων Σχολής Εφαρμοσμένων Μαθηματικών και Φυσικών Επιστημών Εθνικού Μετσόβιου Πολυτεχνείου» (in its Statute's own English version, “School of Applied Mathematics and Physical Sciences Graduates Association, National Technical University of Athens”), with its seat in the Municipality of Zografou, Attica, Greece.

For any matter concerning your personal data, write to us at gradsemfe@gmail.com.

The Association has not appointed a Data Protection Officer (DPO), because it is not required to (Article 37 GDPR): it does not monitor people systematically on a large scale and it does not process special categories of data.

Where this applies

This policy applies to the website semfealumni.gr and its English version, to the account and the membership application, to the Members' area and to the e-mails we send you through the website. It does not apply to third-party websites and services that links from here lead to (e.g. LinkedIn, YouTube, PayPal, Revolut), which have their own policies.

When you are only reading the website

No account is needed. For a page to reach you, your browser connects to the servers below, which receive, as with any website, your IP address and technical details of your browser:

  • GitHub Pages (GitHub, Inc., USA), which hosts the website. GitHub logs visitors' IP addresses for security purposes, in accordance with its privacy statement.
  • Google Fonts (Google), from which the fonts are loaded.
  • YouTube (Google), only on the page that has an embedded video. The video is loaded in privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). If you play it, Google's privacy policy applies and YouTube may store information in your browser.
  • Firebase (Google, from gstatic.com), only when you need it: when you press “Sign in”, on the members' pages, or if you have signed in from this browser before. The What's new and Statistics pages also ask Firebase what has been approved for publication and what the anonymous member statistics are; this happens without cookies and without anything being stored in your browser.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in running the website properly and securely (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Cookies and storage in your browser

We use no cookies. The website keeps in your browser (localStorage, sessionStorage, IndexedDB) only what is strictly necessary for functions you ask for. For these the law does not require consent (Article 4(5) of Greek Law 3471/2006):

  • The language you chose with the flags at the top (localStorage), so that the next pages open in that language.
  • A marker that your visit has already been counted and, on the Statistics page, the period you chose (sessionStorage). They are erased as soon as you close the tab.
  • Your sign-in, if you sign in. Firebase keeps what it needs to keep you signed in (IndexedDB and localStorage). The website keeps your name, so that it appears at the top of the page straight away, and small notes for the sign-in to work (e.g. that you pressed “Sign out”, that your e-mail is awaiting confirmation, or a one-time code during the LinkedIn sign-in). Most of them are erased when you sign out.
  • For administrators only: the draft of an announcement they are writing, so that it is not lost if the page closes.

You can erase them whenever you like from your browser's settings. You will then simply need to sign in or choose your language again.

Visit statistics

To know how many people read the website and from where, we use two counters. The results, only as totals, are published on the Statistics page.

  • Google Analytics 4, without cookies. It stores nothing on your device (no cookie and nothing else), and we have switched off the advertising features and “Google signals”. It sends Google the page you read (without the parameters of its address), the type of your device and the page you came from. From your IP address Google works out the country and the city; according to Google, Google Analytics 4 does not log or store IP addresses. Because there is no identifier on your device, it cannot know whether you are the same visitor as yesterday. Google keeps the detailed data for as long as our account's retention setting says, at most 14 months.
  • Our own counter. Each page sends our server (Google Cloud, in the European Union) a short message: which page you read, whether it is the first page of your visit, and which website you came from. On the first page of each visit, the server looks up which organisation owns the network you are connecting from, by asking the public address registries (the DNS name system and the internet address registries ARIN and RIPE NCC, which receive the address only to answer). It keeps only the name of the university or company, if the network belongs to such an organisation. Home and mobile connections belong to internet providers and are not matched to anyone. We keep only totals per day (for example “12 visits to Announcements”, “2 visits from NTUA”), never a record per visitor. A company is shown publicly only if it has at least two visits in the period.

Our code does not store or log your IP address. As for every request, Google Cloud's infrastructure keeps a technical log that includes the address, for a limited period (normally up to 30 days). We open it only to fix a problem or to deal with abuse.

If your browser asks not to be tracked (Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track), we do not count your visit at all, with either of the two counters. The administration pages and the LinkedIn sign-in page are never counted.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in knowing how many people read the website and from where, so that we can improve it and tell the members about its reach (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Because nothing is stored on your device, no consent is needed under Greek Law 3471/2006. The daily totals do not concern any person and we keep them without a time limit.

Your account

Signing in works through Google's Firebase Authentication service. You can sign in with Google, LinkedIn or e-mail and password. From the provider you choose we receive only:

  • your name,
  • your e-mail address,
  • your profile photo (if there is one), and
  • an identifier of your account with the provider.

We do not receive your Google or LinkedIn password, nor any access to your contacts, posts or messages. If you sign in with e-mail, Firebase stores your password in a form that cannot be reversed (a hash), and it is not visible to anyone, not even to us. Firebase sends you an e-mail to confirm your address; until you confirm it, the account cannot be used. Firebase also keeps when the account was created and when you last signed in.

If you sign in with LinkedIn, we keep a record that links your LinkedIn identifier to your account here, so that we recognise you the next time. It is deleted together with your account.

The website's administrators see a list of all accounts: the name, the e-mail, the ways of signing in, and when each one was created and last signed in. They use it to approve members and to find duplicate accounts of the same person and merge them into one. Every such merge is recorded in a log that only the project's administrators can see in Firebase: which accounts, their names and e-mails, who made the merge and when. We keep this log for security and accountability, and it is not erased automatically when you delete your account; we delete it when it is no longer needed or if you ask us to, unless it must be kept to defend legal claims.

Legal basis: providing the service you ask for, under the terms of use (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR), and our legitimate interest in the security of accounts and in avoiding duplicate registrations (Article 6(1)(f)).

The membership application

If you fill in the membership application, we store the details you give in Google's Cloud Firestore database:

  • Required: first name, last name, e-mail and role (SEMFE graduate, final-year student or faculty member). Without them we cannot consider the application.
  • Optional: phone number, years of admission and graduation, study track, employer and position, industry, city, country, gender, LinkedIn profile, a note to the Board of Directors, and your contact preferences (newsletter, job announcements, members' directory). If you do not give them, your application is not affected.
  • What the Board of Directors records: the status of the application (under review, active, rejected) and the years for which the membership fee has been paid.

We use them to:

  • confirm that you are entitled to become a member, as the Statute (PDF, in Greek) provides,
  • keep the register of members and the membership fees,
  • send you what concerns your membership (e.g. invitations to general assemblies and elections, fee reminders),
  • send you the newsletter and announcements of jobs and internships, only if you choose to,
  • produce the anonymous member statistics on the Statistics page.

For the statistics we count only totals per question (e.g. “12 members in Athens”), never two answers of the same person together and never a name or an e-mail. A group of fewer than 3 people is not shown by name (it goes into “Other”), a question is shown only when at least 5 people have answered it, and years are counted in five-year periods. The answer “I prefer not to say” is not counted anywhere.

We do not ask for special categories of data (e.g. about health or political or religious beliefs). Please do not write such details in your note or in your messages.

Legal basis: your relationship with the Association as a member, under its Statute (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR), for the required details, the register and the fees; your consent (Article 6(1)(a)) for the optional details and the contact preferences, which you can change or erase at any time; and our legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)) for the anonymous statistics. The box in the application with which you state that you have read this policy confirms that you have been informed; it is not the only basis of the processing.

Your details are seen by you and by the members of the Board of Directors who manage the register of members. We do not sell them and we do not give them to third parties.

Membership fees and donations

Payments are not made on the website but through PayPal (PayPal (Europe) S.à r.l. et Cie, S.C.A., Luxembourg) or by bank transfer or through the Revolut app to the account at Revolut Bank UAB (Lithuania) shown on the Membership and Donations page. The website does not see or store card or bank account details. These providers process the payment data as independent controllers, under their own policies.

From each payment we receive the payer's name, the amount, the date, the payment reference and, if you give it, your e-mail address. We use them to record your membership fee in the register of members, to keep the Association's financial records, and, if you give us contact details with a donation, to send you a letter of thanks. The financial reports we publish show only totals of income and expenses and do not name members or donors.

Legal basis: your membership (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR), the Association's legal obligation to keep financial and accounting records (Article 6(1)(c)), and our legitimate interest in thanking those who support us (Article 6(1)(f)).

The members' directory

If you choose to, a short profile of you (full name, year of graduation, study track, job, city, LinkedIn) appears in the directory of the Members' area, which only active members of the Association can see. The members who see it may use it only for communication between members, under the terms of use. You can withdraw your profile at any time from your account.

Legal basis: your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR), which you can withdraw at any time.

Alerts, newsletter and job announcements

If you choose e-mail alerts (announcements of the Association, events and meetings, website news), we keep which ones you chose and your account's e-mail address, so that we can send you a short e-mail when something new of these is published. The e-mails go only to your confirmed sign-in address. Every e-mail has a link that stops them all with one button, and you can change your choices whenever you like from your account. They are deleted together with your account.

In the same way, we send you the newsletter and announcements of jobs and internships only if you chose them in the membership application, and they stop as soon as you untick them.

These e-mails are sent from the Association's e-mail account on Google's Gmail. The legal basis is your consent (Article 6(1)(a) GDPR and Article 11 of Greek Law 3471/2006). Withdrawing it does not affect what was sent before.

Feedback, problem reports and correspondence

If you write to us from the Feedback and problems page, we keep your message, the screenshots you attached, the page it concerns, the type of your browser and your account details (name and sign-in e-mail), so that we can look into it and reply to you. We send you e-mails only at your account's address and only if it is confirmed: a confirmation with the number of your message and, later, our reply.

The messages are seen by the website's administrators, who also receive a copy by e-mail. A copy of them is also kept in a private repository on GitHub, where we read them in order to fix the problems. Our answer may be recorded in the website's public repository with the number of your message, never with your name or e-mail.

When you delete your account, your messages are deleted from the database and removed from the copy on GitHub at the next synchronisation (within a short time). Older versions may remain in the history of the private repository and in the administrators' mailboxes; we delete them if you ask us to. The same applies to e-mails you send us directly: we keep them for as long as we need to reply to you and to have a record of our correspondence, and we delete them if you ask us to, unless they must be kept to defend legal claims.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in replying to those who write to us and in improving the website (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR).

Announcements, photographs and people on the website

The website publishes information about the Association's activities, which may include personal data: the names of the members of its bodies, the names of speakers and participants in events, photographs and videos from events (in the Photo gallery and in announcements), and documents from its archive (e.g. financial reports signed by members of the Board of Directors).

The legal basis is the Association's legitimate interest in informing its members and the public about its activities and in keeping its history (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR), and, for the members of its bodies, the transparency that running an association requires. Where needed, we ask for consent.

If you appear in a photograph or are mentioned on a page and want to be removed, write to us. We will remove or blur the photograph or your name without undue delay, unless there is a serious reason for it to stay (e.g. a document the Association must keep). Anything published on the internet may already have been copied by third parties (e.g. search engines or web archives), which we do not control.

When an administrator publishes, changes or deletes an announcement from the website, their e-mail address is recorded in the change history of the website's public repository on GitHub.

Who sees your data

  • The Board of Directors and the website's administrators, only as far as their duties require. They must keep the data confidential and use it only for the purposes of this policy.
  • Other active members, only your profile in the members' directory, if you choose it.
  • The service providers we use: Google (Firebase Authentication, Cloud Firestore, Cloud Functions, Google Analytics, Google Fonts, Gmail) and GitHub (hosting of the website and a private copy of the messages). For the Firebase and Google Cloud services Google acts as a processor on our behalf, under the Firebase data processing terms, and for Google Analytics under Google's data processing terms.
  • Third parties acting as independent controllers, when you use them: the provider you sign in with (Google or LinkedIn), PayPal and Revolut for payments, YouTube for videos, and the public address registries for the visit counter.
  • Public authorities or courts, only when the law obliges us.

We do not sell, rent or give your details to third parties for advertising or for any other purpose.

Where it is stored and transfers outside the EU

The database (Cloud Firestore) and our server functions (Cloud Functions) are in Google data centres in the European Union. Some services, such as Firebase Authentication, Google Analytics, Gmail and GitHub, may also process data in countries outside the European Economic Area, in particular in the USA.

These transfers take place with the safeguards of Articles 45 and 46 GDPR: the European Commission's adequacy decision for the EU-US Data Privacy Framework (Implementing Decision (EU) 2023/1795), in which Google LLC and GitHub, Inc. participate, and the Commission's standard contractual clauses (Implementing Decision (EU) 2021/914) included in the providers' data processing terms. We will send you a copy of the safeguards if you ask us.

The network lookup of the visit counter may pass through the server of the ARIN registry in the USA, which receives only the IP address in order to answer and no other information about you.

Legal bases at a glance

  • Providing the service and membership (Article 6(1)(b) GDPR): the account, the required details of the application, the register of members, the membership fees and communication on Association matters.
  • Consent (Article 6(1)(a)): the optional details, the members' directory, the alerts, the newsletter and the job announcements. You can withdraw it at any time.
  • Legal obligation (Article 6(1)(c)): the financial and accounting records, and replying to lawful requests from authorities.
  • Legitimate interest (Article 6(1)(f)): the secure operation of the website, the visit statistics, merging duplicate accounts, replying to feedback, informing people about the Association's activities with names and photographs, thanking donors, and defending legal claims. In each case we have weighed that this interest does not override your rights, and you can object (see Right to object).

How long we keep the data

  • Account, membership application, directory, alerts and feedback messages: for as long as you have an account. They are deleted as soon as you delete it.
  • Financial and accounting records of membership fees and donations: for as long as accounting and tax legislation requires (normally five years from the end of the year they concern, or longer if the law so requires).
  • Register of members: if you have been a member, the Association may keep in its register and its minutes only the minimum details that prove your membership and your participation in its bodies and votes (e.g. full name and period of membership), for as long as its operation as an association requires.
  • Log of account merges: for as long as needed for security and accountability (see Your account).
  • Correspondence: for as long as needed to reply to you and to have a record of our correspondence.
  • Technical logs of the providers: for a few weeks (at Google Cloud normally up to 30 days).
  • Backups of the providers: data you deleted may remain in them for a limited period (according to Google, up to about six months) until they are overwritten. They are not used for any other purpose.
  • Anonymous statistics: without a time limit, since they do not concern any person.
  • Details needed for legal claims: for as long as the dispute lasts and until the related claims are time-barred.

Security

We take appropriate technical and organisational measures to protect your data (Article 32 GDPR). For example: all connections are encrypted (HTTPS), Google encrypts the data on its disks, the database's access rules let each person see only what they are entitled to (their own details and, for active members, the directory), passwords are never stored in readable form, and only a few authorised people have administrator access. No system is completely secure; that is why we keep only what is necessary.

If a data breach occurs that may put your rights at risk, we will notify the Hellenic Data Protection Authority within 72 hours of becoming aware of it and, if the risk is high, we will also inform you without delay (Articles 33 and 34 GDPR).

Minors

The accounts and the Members' area are intended for graduates, final-year students and faculty members of SEMFE NTUA, that is, for adults. People under 18 must not create an account. If we learn that a minor's account has been created, we will delete it together with its details.

Automated decisions

We do not make decisions about you by automated means alone and we do not profile you (Article 22 GDPR). Membership applications are considered by people, the members of the Board of Directors.

Your rights

You have the following rights (Articles 15 to 21 GDPR):

  • Access: to find out whether we process data about you and to get a copy of it.
  • Rectification: to correct or complete inaccurate or incomplete details.
  • Erasure: to ask for your data to be deleted (see Data deletion).
  • Restriction: to ask us to stop the processing temporarily, e.g. while we check its accuracy.
  • Portability: to get the details you gave us in a common, machine-readable format or to ask for them to be sent to someone else.
  • Objection: see the next section.
  • Withdrawal of consent: where we rely on your consent, you can withdraw it at any time, without affecting what was done before (Article 7(3)).

You can exercise most of them yourself, immediately, from the My account page. For anything else, write to us at gradsemfe@gmail.com, preferably from your account's address. If we need to make sure that it is you, we will ask only for what is strictly necessary.

We will reply without undue delay and at the latest within one month. If the request is complex or there are many requests, this period may be extended by two further months; we will tell you so within the first month. Exercising your rights is free of charge, unless a request is manifestly unfounded or excessive (Article 12 GDPR). If we cannot grant a request, for example because the law obliges us to keep certain details, we will explain why. The rights are exercised subject to the restrictions provided by the GDPR and Greek Law 4624/2019.

Right to object

Where we process your data on the basis of our legitimate interest (e.g. visit statistics, photographs and names in announcements, thanks for donations), you have the right to object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation (Article 21 GDPR). We will then stop the processing, unless there are compelling legitimate grounds that override your interests and rights, or we need it to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

For the visit statistics it is enough to switch on Global Privacy Control or Do Not Track in your browser. The e-mails we send you because you asked for them (alerts, newsletter, job announcements) can be stopped at any time, without giving any reason.

Complaint to the Authority

If you consider that the processing of your data breaches the law, you have the right to lodge a complaint with the Hellenic Data Protection Authority (1-3 Kifisias Avenue, 115 23 Athens, Greece, tel. +30 210 6475600, www.dpa.gr) or with the supervisory authority of the Member State where you live or work (Article 77 GDPR). We would appreciate it, however, if you wrote to us first, so that we can try to resolve it together.

Changes to this policy

We may change this policy, for example when we add something new to the website or when the law changes. The new version is published on this page with a new date. For significant changes we will also let you know with an announcement on the website or by e-mail. If a change needs your consent, we will ask you for it.
